## 1. The Anatomy of an AP Human Geography FRQ

On the AP Human Geography exam and in Mrs. Knight's classroom, an FRQ is **not** an English essay.
* ❌ **Do NOT write an introduction or conclusion.** You earn zero points for intro or summary paragraphs.
* ❌ **Do NOT use bullet points.** College Board strictly requires complete sentences.
* ✅ **Label each part clearly:** Write **Part A:**, **Part B:**, **Part C:**, etc.
* ✅ **Obey the Task Verbs:** The point is awarded solely on whether you satisfied the command verb.

### The 3 Essential College Board Task Verbs:
1. **`IDENTIFY` (1 Point):** 
   * *What to do:* Name or state a specific geographic fact, country, model stage, or pattern.
   * *Length:* 1 direct, complete sentence. No explanation needed.
   * *Example:* *"Country X is in Stage 2 of the Demographic Transition Model."*
2. **`DESCRIBE` (1 Point):** 
   * *What to do:* Provide the physical or structural characteristics of a trend or phenomenon (what does it look like / what are its features?).
   * *Length:* 2 complete sentences.
   * *Example:* *"Stage 2 of the DTM is characterized by a high Crude Birth Rate and a rapidly dropping Crude Death Rate, producing an expansive pyramid with a wide base of youth cohorts."*
3. **`EXPLAIN` (1 Point):** 
   * *What to do:* Provide the *how*, *why*, or *consequence*. You **MUST** establish a cause-and-effect relationship.
   * *Magic Formula:* Use transition words: **"because..."**, **"which leads to..."**, or **"as a result of..."**
   * *Length:* 2 to 4 complete sentences.
   * *Example:* *"The Crude Death Rate declines rapidly in Stage 2 **because** the diffusion of modern antibiotics, clean municipal water systems, and pasteurization prevents infant waterborne diseases, **which results in** a dramatic drop in infant mortality while birth rates remain culturally high."*

## 2. The 5 High-Yield FRQ Scenarios for Tuesday, Oct 20

Mrs. Knight's Unit 2 FRQ Formative will test one or more of these 5 classic stimulus scenarios:

---

### 📊 Scenario 1: Population Pyramids & Dependency Ratios (Topics 2.3 & 2.9)
* **Visual Stimulus:** Two contrasting population pyramids:
  * **Country X (e.g., Nigeria / Uganda):** Expansive, broad triangle base (ages 0–14), narrow apex.
  * **Country Y (e.g., Japan / Germany):** Constrictive, beehive/inverted pyramid, narrow base, wide middle/top (ages 55+).
* **High-Yield Questions & Winning Answers:**
  * **Identify DTM Stages:** Country X is **Stage 2** (high birth rate, rapid growth). Country Y is **Stage 4 or 5** (birth rate at or below replacement level 2.1).
  * **Describe Dependency Ratios:** 
    * Country X has a high **youth dependency ratio** (large percentage of children under 15 dependent on a smaller working-age workforce).
    * Country Y has a high **elderly dependency ratio** (large percentage of retired citizens aged 65+ dependent on shrinking working-age taxpayers).
  * **Explain Economic Challenge for Country X (Young):** High government expenditure required for pediatric clinics, childhood vaccinations, and primary schools, diverting state capital away from industrial infrastructure.
  * **Explain Economic Challenge for Country Y (Aging):** Severe labor shortages, shrinking income tax base, and surging government costs for public pensions and geriatric medical care.
  * **Explain Policy Response:** Country Y can introduce **pro-natalist policies** (paid parental leave, subsidized childcare, baby bonuses) or expand international labor immigration to replenish the workforce.

---

### 📈 Scenario 2: The Demographic & Epidemiological Transition Models (Topics 2.5 & 2.6)
* **Visual Stimulus:** The 5-Stage DTM graph showing Crude Birth Rate (CBR), Crude Death Rate (CDR), and Natural Increase Rate (NIR).
* **High-Yield Questions & Winning Answers:**
  * **Explain why CDR drops before CBR in Stage 2:** 
    * Medical technology and clean water can be imported quickly (**Medical Revolution** in LDCs / **Industrial Revolution** in Europe), immediately reducing mortality.
    * In contrast, cultural values favoring large families (agrarian labor, religious norms, lack of female education) change slowly across generations.
  * **Explain why CBR drops sharply in Stage 3:** 
    * Urbanization makes children an economic cost rather than farm labor.
    * Women enter secondary education and the formal workforce, delaying marriage and childbearing.
    * Increased access to family planning and contraception.
  * **Connect to the Epidemiological Transition Model (ETM):**
    * *Stage 1:* Pestilence and famine (Black Plague, cholera, infectious waterborne diseases).
    * *Stage 2:* Receding pandemics (improved sanitation and antibiotics).
    * *Stage 3 & 4:* Degenerative and lifestyle diseases (cardiovascular heart disease, cancer, stroke, diabetes).
    * *Stage 5 (Possible Reemergence):* Drug-resistant bacteria (MRSA, drug-resistant malaria) due to antibiotic overuse and global airline connectivity.

---

### 🗺️ Scenario 3: Distance Decay, Ravenstein's Laws & Global Migration Streams (Topic 2.10 & 2.11)
* **Visual Stimulus:** Map showing global migration arrows paired with a downward-sloping distance decay curve.
* **High-Yield Questions & Winning Answers:**
  * **Define Distance Decay:** The geographic principle stating that as the spatial distance between two locations increases, the volume of interaction (migration) between them decreases. Most migrants travel short distances.
  * **Explain a Real-World Migration Flow Illustrating Distance Decay:** 
    * *Mexico/Central America to the US:* High migration volume occurs across a shared contiguous land border.
    * *Syria to Jordan/Lebanon/Turkey:* Syrian refugees overwhelmingly sought shelter in neighboring countries rather than traveling across continents.
  * **Apply Ravenstein's Laws:**
    1. Most migrants move a short distance.
    2. Long-distance migrants typically head to major commercial/industrial centers.
    3. Most long-distance migrants historically are young single adults, not family units.
    4. Every migration flow produces a counterflow.
    5. Urban residents are less migratory than rural residents.

---

### 🌍 Scenario 4: Refugees, IDPs & The Syrian / Venezuelan Crises (Topic 2.11)
* **Visual Stimulus:** Map of Middle East or Latin America showing refugee flow vectors.
* **High-Yield Questions & Winning Answers:**
  * **Distinguish Refugee vs. Internally Displaced Person (IDP):**
    * A **Refugee** has fled across an **international border** due to a well-founded fear of persecution, conflict, or violence, and is protected under international law (UNHCR).
    * An **IDP** has fled for the same reasons but remains **within their country's national borders**.
  * **Explain Political Push Factors (Syria):** Aerial bombardment by the Assad regime, barrel bombs, ISIS terrorism, and destruction of urban housing in Aleppo and Damascus.
  * **Explain Environmental/Economic Push Factors:** The severe 2006–2011 Syrian drought decimated agricultural crops, forcing rural farmers into urban squatter settlements before the civil war broke out.

---

### 💰 Scenario 5: Dual Consequences of Migration (Topic 2.12)
* **High-Yield Questions & Winning Answers:**
  * **Consequence on Country of Origin (Sending Country):**
    * *Positive:* **Remittances** — Migrants wire billions of dollars back home, which directly funds family healthcare, nutrition, and schooling, injecting capital into developing economies.
    * *Negative:* **Brain Drain** — Emigration of highly skilled professionals (doctors, engineers, professors) deprives origin countries of the human capital needed to build hospitals and institutions.
  * **Consequence on Destination Country (Receiving Country):**
    * *Positive:* **Labor Deficit Relief & Brain Gain** — Migrants fill vital labor shortages in agriculture, construction, and healthcare, while lowering median age and generating payroll taxes to support aging retirees.
    * *Negative:* **Infrastructure Strain & Social Tension** — Local school districts and hospitals experience budget strain; cultural differences can occasionally trigger localized **xenophobia** or political backlash.

## 3. High-Scoring Step-by-Step Writing Template for Tuesday

When you receive the prompt sheet in class on Tuesday:

1. **Step 1 (First 2 Minutes): Read & Circle Task Verbs.**
   * Circle `IDENTIFY`, `DESCRIBE`, `EXPLAIN`.
2. **Step 2: Answer in Order and Label Clearly.**
   * Write:
     ```text
     Part A: [Direct 1-sentence identification]
     Part B: [2-sentence description of characteristics]
     Part C: [2-3 sentence explanation with "because" / "which results in"]
     Part D: [2-3 sentence explanation with specific real-world example]
     Part E: [2-3 sentence explanation of policy or consequence]
     ```
3. **Step 3: Always Use Specific Geographic Vocabulary.**
   * Don't say *"people had fewer babies."*  
     👉 Say: *"Total Fertility Rate (TFR) dropped below the replacement level of 2.1 due to female workforce participation."*
   * Don't say *"they sent money back home."*  
     👉 Say: *"Migrants send financial **remittances**, boosting household capital in origin nations."*
   * Don't say *"smart people left."*  
     👉 Say: *"The origin country experiences severe **Brain Drain** as trained physicians emigrate for higher salaries."*
