High-Yield Prompt 1: Population Pyramids & Dependency Ratios

Topics 2.3 & 2.9 • Demographic Structure and Ageing Populations

7 Points
Stimulus Scenario: Country X (e.g., Nigeria) possesses an expansive population pyramid with a broad base of children under 15 and a rapidly tapering apex. Country Y (e.g., Japan) exhibits a constrictive pyramid with an inverted, narrow base and a wide bulge in cohorts aged 55 and older.
Part A (1 pt) — IDENTIFY DTM Stage 1 Sentence

Identify the stage of the Demographic Transition Model most characteristic of Country X.

College Board Model Answer: Country X is in Stage 2 of the Demographic Transition Model (characterized by high crude birth rates, rapidly plummeting death rates, and explosive natural population increase).
Part B (1 pt) — DESCRIBE Dependency Ratio 2 Sentences

Describe the primary difference in dependency ratios between Country X and Country Y.

College Board Model Answer: Country X has a high youth dependency ratio, meaning a disproportionately large percentage of children under age 15 depend on a smaller working-age population. In contrast, Country Y has a high elderly dependency ratio, meaning a large retired population aged 65 and older depends on a shrinking workforce.
Part C (1 pt) — EXPLAIN Economic Challenge (Youth) Must use "Because"

Explain ONE economic challenge faced by Country X as a result of its demographic structure.

College Board Model Answer: Country X experiences severe fiscal strain because a rapidly growing youth cohort requires massive state capital for primary schools, teacher hiring, and pediatric vaccinations, which diverts tax revenues away from building industrial infrastructure and transport networks.
Part D (1 pt) — EXPLAIN Economic Challenge (Aging) Must use "Because"

Explain ONE economic challenge faced by Country Y as a result of its demographic structure.

College Board Model Answer: Country Y faces severe labor deficits because an aging and shrinking workforce generates declining income tax revenues while the national cost of funding public pensions and long-term geriatric medical care escalates rapidly.
Part E (1 pt) — EXPLAIN Pro-Natalist Policy Cause-Effect Link

Explain ONE policy Country Y could implement to address its population decline.

College Board Model Answer: Country Y could implement pro-natalist policies such as state-subsidized daycare, extended paid maternal/paternal leave, or direct cash baby bonuses, which lowers the financial barrier to childrearing and incentivizes families to increase their fertility rate closer to the 2.1 replacement level.

High-Yield Prompt 2: DTM & Epidemiological Transition (ETM)

Topics 2.5 & 2.6 • Disease Patterns & Demographic Shifts

7 Points

Part A (1 pt) — Describe Birth Rate Shift (Stage 2 to 3)

Model Answer: In Stage 2, crude birth rates remain extremely high, whereas in Stage 3, birth rates drop sharply as society urbanizes, women enter higher education and careers, and family planning becomes widespread.

Part B (1 pt) — Explain Why CDR Falls Before CBR in Stage 2

Model Answer: The death rate declines first because medical innovations (antibiotics, vaccines, clean municipal water) can be quickly diffused and adopted to immediately stop infant deaths, whereas cultural and religious values encouraging large families change much more slowly over multiple generations.

Part C (1 pt) — Describe Causes of Death in ETM Stage 1 vs. Stage 4

Model Answer: Stage 1 is dominated by infectious parasitic diseases, famines, and pestilence (e.g., cholera, Black Plague), whereas Stage 4 is dominated by chronic, degenerative, non-communicable lifestyle conditions (e.g., cardiovascular heart disease, stroke, and cancer).

Part D (1 pt) — Explain ETM Stage 5 Reemergence

Model Answer: Infectious diseases can reemerge in a potential Stage 5 because bacteria and pathogens evolve genetic resistance to standard antibiotics (e.g., MRSA, drug-resistant tuberculosis), which allows them to spread globally in hours via international airline transit networks.

High-Yield Prompt 3: Distance Decay, Ravenstein & Refugees

Topics 2.10, 2.11 & 2.12 • Migration Dynamics and Consequences

7 Points

Part A (1 pt) — Define Distance Decay in Migration

Model Answer: Distance decay is the geographic principle stating that as the physical distance between two places increases, the volume of spatial interaction (migration) between them decreases. Most migrants travel only short distances.

Part B (1 pt) — Distinguish Refugee vs. IDP

Model Answer: A refugee has fled their home due to conflict or persecution and has crossed an international border, whereas an internally displaced person (IDP) has fled for the same reasons but remains within their own nation's borders.

Part C (1 pt) — Explain Remittances (Positive Impact on Origin)

Model Answer: Emigration benefits sending countries because migrants working abroad wire financial remittances back to their families, which increases household purchasing power for food, medical care, and education, stimulating local economies in developing nations.

Part D (1 pt) — Explain Brain Drain (Negative Impact on Origin)

Model Answer: Emigration harms sending countries because it causes Brain Drain, wherein the most educated physicians, engineers, and educators leave for higher salaries abroad, leaving the origin nation with critical shortages of professional human capital to staff hospitals and schools.
