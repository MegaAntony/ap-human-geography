Master the College Board task verbs (Identify, Describe, Explain) across high-yield Unit 2 topics: Population Pyramids, DTM/ETM transitions, Malthusian theories, and Migration push/pull policies.

IDENTIFY (1 Point)

Simply state or name the geographic concept or feature. Write 1 complete, direct sentence. No detailed explanation or reasoning is required.

DESCRIBE (1 Point)

Provide the characteristics, attributes, or "what does it look like." Write 2–3 sentences illustrating the structure, pattern, or process.

EXPLAIN (1 Point)

Provide the how, why, or consequence. Always use causal transition words: "Because...", "This leads to...", "As a result..." to connect the cause to the demographic effect.

FRQ Practice 1: Population Pyramids & Dependency Ratios

Topics 2.3 & 2.9 • 7 Points
Stimulus Context: Country X exhibits an expansive population pyramid with a very wide base of children aged 0–14 and a narrow apex. Country Y exhibits a constrictive, inverted pyramid with a narrow base and a wide bulge in cohorts aged 55+.

Part A (1 pt): Identify the DTM stage most characteristic of Country X.

Model Answer: Country X is characteristic of Stage 2 of the Demographic Transition Model (high birth rate, rapidly falling death rate, and rapid natural increase).

Part B (1 pt): Describe the dependency ratio for Country X compared to Country Y.

Model Answer: Country X has a high youth dependency ratio (large proportion of children under 15 dependent on the working-age population), whereas Country Y has a high elderly dependency ratio (large proportion of retired citizens aged 65 and older dependent on the workforce).

Part C (1 pt): Explain ONE economic challenge faced by Country X due to its demographic structure.

Model Answer: Country X faces heavy economic strain because a rapidly growing youth cohort requires massive government investment in primary schools, pediatric healthcare, and infant vaccinations, which diverts capital away from industrial infrastructure and economic development projects.

Part D (1 pt): Explain ONE economic challenge faced by Country Y due to its demographic structure.

Model Answer: Country Y faces a labor shortage and fiscal strain because a shrinking working-age cohort generates fewer income tax revenues while the cost of public pension systems and long-term geriatric medical care increases dramatically as the population ages.

Part E (1 pt): Explain ONE pro-natalist policy that Country Y could adopt to address its population decline.

Model Answer: Country Y could implement subsidized childcare programs or direct cash baby bonuses for parents, which lowers the financial cost of raising children and encourages young families to have more offspring, thereby raising the Total Fertility Rate (TFR) closer to the replacement level of 2.1.

FRQ Practice 2: Demographic & Epidemiological Transition Models

Topics 2.5 & 2.6 • 7 Points

Part A (1 pt): Describe the primary difference in birth rates (CBR) between Stage 2 and Stage 3 of the DTM.

Model Answer: In Stage 2, the crude birth rate remains very high, whereas in Stage 3, the birth rate drops sharply due to urbanization, increased female education, and widespread family planning.

Part B (1 pt): Explain why the crude death rate (CDR) declines rapidly in Stage 2 before the birth rate (CBR) falls.

Model Answer: CDR drops rapidly in Stage 2 because the diffusion of medical technology, penicillin/antibiotics, clean municipal water systems, and pasteurization immediately reduces infant mortality and infectious diseases, while cultural and religious attitudes favoring large families change much more slowly over several generations.

Part C (1 pt): Describe the leading causes of death in Stage 1 versus Stage 3/4 of the Epidemiological Transition Model.

Model Answer: Stage 1 is dominated by infectious parasitic diseases, famines, and pandemics (e.g., Black Plague), while Stage 3 and 4 are dominated by chronic, degenerative, non-communicable lifestyle diseases (e.g., cardiovascular disease and cancer).

Part D (1 pt): Explain ONE factor contributing to a possible Stage 5 reemergence of infectious diseases.

Model Answer: A possible Stage 5 reemergence occurs because pathogens mutate and evolve resistance to common antibiotics and pesticides (e.g., drug-resistant malaria or MRSA), which renders standard pharmaceutical treatments ineffective and allows infectious diseases to spread again in connected global transit hubs.

FRQ Practice 3: Migration Push/Pull, Ravenstein & Remittances

Topics 2.10, 2.11 & 2.12 • 7 Points

Part A (1 pt): Describe the legal distinction between a refugee and an internally displaced person (IDP).

Model Answer: A refugee is someone who has been forced to flee their home due to conflict or persecution and has crossed an international border, whereas an internally displaced person (IDP) has fled for the same reasons but remains within their own country's national borders.

Part B (1 pt): Explain ONE positive economic impact of emigration on the origin country.

Model Answer: Emigration benefits the origin country economically because migrants working abroad send remittances (direct financial transfers) back to their families, which increases household purchasing power for food, healthcare, and education, stimulating local economies in developing nations.

Part C (1 pt): Explain ONE negative economic impact of emigration on the origin country.

Model Answer: Emigration harms the origin country because it causes Brain Drain, wherein the most educated professionals (doctors, engineers, researchers) emigrate to developed countries for higher salaries, leaving the origin nation with severe shortages of skilled labor to build critical social infrastructure.
