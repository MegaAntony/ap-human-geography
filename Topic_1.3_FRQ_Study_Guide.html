🛰️ Section 1: Core Geospatial Technologies (Student pp. 23–25 / Key p. 3)
TOPIC 1.3 • GEOGRAPHIC INFORMATION SYSTEMS (GIS) Define
📖 Student Book: Page 23 (Geospatial Data Sources) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Define Geographic Information Systems (GIS).
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"GIS (Geographic Information Systems) is a computer software system designed to capture, store, manipulate, analyze, and display multiple layers of geographically referenced spatial data."
💡 Memory Hook: GIS = Geographic Information Software (Smart digital map with layered data).
TOPIC 1.3 • GIS DATA LAYERING Explain
📖 Student Book: Pages 23–24 (Benefits of Geospatial Data) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Explain the concept of "data layering" in GIS and how it assists urban and environmental planners.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Data layering involves overlaying distinct thematic map layers (such as flood zones, soil types, elevation, and population density) on the exact same coordinate grid to identify spatial relationships, hazard risks, and optimal building sites."
💡 Memory Hook: Stack transparent sheets: Layer 1 (Elevation) + Layer 2 (Flood zones) + Layer 3 (Homes) $\rightarrow$ Identifies who is at risk!
TOPIC 1.3 • GLOBAL POSITIONING SYSTEM (GPS) Describe
📖 Student Book: Page 23 (GPS & Navigation) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Describe the primary function of the Global Positioning System (GPS).
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"GPS uses a constellation of orbiting satellites transmitting radio signals to receivers on Earth to calculate exact absolute location (latitude, longitude, and elevation) and provide real-time navigation tracking."
💡 Memory Hook: GPS = Gives you your exact coordinates on Earth anywhere, anytime.
TOPIC 1.3 • REMOTE SENSING Describe
📖 Student Book: Page 23 (Remote Sensing & Satellites) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Describe how "remote sensing" collects geographic data.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Remote sensing collects physical and environmental data by using cameras, radar, or infrared sensors on satellites, aircraft, or drones to scan Earth's surface from a distance without direct physical contact."
💡 Memory Hook: Remote = Far away. Sensing = Scanning from above (monitoring melting polar ice, forest fires, crop health).
🌍 Section 2: Student Book Figures & Decision-Making Case Studies (Student pp. 23–25 / Key p. 3)
STUDENT BOOK IN-TEXT FIGURE Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 23 (Water Pump in Africa Image) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Explain why community involvement and local qualitative knowledge are essential when experts use geographic data to solve local problems (such as installing water pumps in developing villages).
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Local community members understand the cultural traditions, sacred sites, and social customs that must be preserved when placing infrastructure; cooperating with locals fosters a sense of community ownership and ensures long-term maintenance of the project."
💡 Memory Hook: Outside experts bring the technology; local villagers know the culture and keep the pump working!
STUDENT BOOK IN-TEXT PERSPECTIVE Describe
📖 Student Book: Page 24 (Geographic Perspectives: London Subway Map) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Describe why the simplified London Subway Map is effective despite containing geographic distance inaccuracies.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"The London Subway map emphasizes relative location and line connectivity in a simple, readable format; by eliminating real-world curves and true geographic distances, it allows commuters to easily navigate transit transfers."
💡 Memory Hook: Subway riders care about *which stop connects to what line*, not exact miles underground!
🏢 Section 3: Commercial Uses, Emergency Response & Privacy Issues (Student pp. 23–25 / Key p. 3)
TOPIC 1.3 • COMMERCIAL & BUSINESS APPLICATION Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 24 (Business Uses of Geospatial Data) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Explain ONE commercial or private business application of GIS technology.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"A retail corporation (such as Target or Starbucks) uses GIS to map customer household income, traffic congestion, and competitor locations within a 15-minute drive-time buffer to identify the most profitable location for a new store."
💡 Memory Hook: GIS drive-time analysis helps big stores find where the wealthiest customers drive past every day.
TOPIC 1.3 • VGI & DISASTER RESPONSE Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 23 (Smartphones & Crowdsourcing) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 3 (PDF Page 11)
Explain how Volunteered Geographic Information (VGI) / crowdsourced data assists emergency disaster response.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Everyday citizens use smartphones to share live spatial reports (via apps like Waze or social media) about road closures, flooded bridges, and downed power lines, providing emergency dispatchers with real-time situational awareness during a natural disaster."
💡 Memory Hook: VGI = Volunteers using GPS on smartphones to report live hazards during hurricanes or floods.
TOPIC 1.3 • PRIVACY & ETHICS CONCERN Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 24 (Tech Companies & Locational Data) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Explain ONE privacy or ethical concern related to the increasing use of geospatial location data.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Continuous location tracking by smartphones and mobile apps enables tech corporations and governments to monitor citizens' daily spatial movements and habits, raising serious concerns regarding unauthorized surveillance and personal privacy loss."
💡 Memory Hook: Your phone tracks everywhere you go—leading to corporate ad tracking and surveillance risks!
