### Question 1 (1.2-Q01)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 1.A` — *Describe geographic concepts, processes, models, and theories.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Quantitative vs. Qualitative Geographic Data (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 1.2, under 'Types of Geographic Data: Quantitative and Qualitative'*)

> **Prompt:** Which of the following data-gathering approaches produces quantitative geospatial data rather than qualitative geographic information?

- **(A)** Compiling census enumeration records of household median income levels across metropolitan tracts.
- **(B)** Recording ethnographic oral histories from long-term senior residents living in historic urban areas.
- **(C)** Transcribing descriptive field journal entries written by nineteenth-century scientific surveyors.
- **(D)** Conducting open-ended unstructured interviews regarding community feelings about neighborhood crime.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Summary Rationale:** Quantitative geographic data consists of numerical measurements, counts, and statistical values (such as census income figures, population counts, or temperature readings) that can be analyzed mathematically.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** Oral histories provide qualitative narrative accounts, personal memories, and cultural descriptions, not numerical geospatial metrics.
  - **(C):** Surveyor journals represent qualitative descriptive text documenting visual impressions rather than quantifiable measurements.
  - **(D):** Open-ended sentiment interviews capture qualitative human perceptions, attitudes, and emotions rather than numerical values.
</details>

  - **(A):** Zoning regulations are enacted and administered by local municipal and county governments, not by the federal decennial census.
  - **(C):** Property tax assessments are calculated by local municipal and county tax assessors based on local real estate values.
