### Question 1 (7.2-Q01)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 1.A` — *Describe geographic concepts, processes, models, and theories.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** The Primary Sector: Resource Extraction (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 7.2, under 'Five Economic Sectors: Primary Sector'*)

> **Prompt:** A worker employed in commercial copper extraction in Chile's Atacama Desert is operating within which economic sector?

- **(A)** The primary sector, which extracts or harvests natural raw materials directly from the earth.
- **(B)** The secondary sector, which fabricates and manufactures finished consumer merchandise in factories.
- **(C)** The tertiary sector, which sells specialized consumer retail merchandise and commercial services.
- **(D)** The quaternary sector, which processes algorithmic spatial databases and scientific information.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Summary Rationale:** The primary economic sector involves the direct harvesting, gathering, or extraction of raw natural resources from the Earth, including mining, commercial agriculture, commercial fishing, and forestry.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** The secondary sector processes, refines, and manufactures extracted raw materials into value-added finished goods.
  - **(C):** The tertiary sector provides consumer, commercial, and professional services such as retail, banking, and hospitality.
  - **(D):** The quaternary sector handles knowledge-based, research, and data-processing tasks such as software development.
</details>

> **Prompt:** Why do commercial copper smelting operations consistently establish processing facilities adjacent to copper ore mines?

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**

  - **(B):** Smelting is a weight-losing process, not bulk-gaining; raw ore contains over 95% waste rock.
  - **(C):** Copper is an imperishable metal and does not spoil during transit.
  - **(D):** Refining extracts pure copper and discards bulk slag, greatly decreasing rather than increasing mass.
