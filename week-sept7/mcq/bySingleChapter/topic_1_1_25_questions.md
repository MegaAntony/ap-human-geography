### Question 1 (1.1-Q01)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 3.A` — *Identify the different types of data presented in visual sources.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Types of Maps: Graduated Symbol Maps (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 1.1, under 'Thematic Maps: Graduated Symbol Maps'*)

> **Prompt:** A cartographer produces a map of European population distribution where each green circle indicates a town or city, and larger circles denote progressively greater municipal populations. Which category of thematic map is utilized in this visual?

- **(A)** A graduated symbol map that scales the size of circular glyphs to data values.
- **(B)** An isoline map that connects points of equal elevation using continuous contour lines.
- **(C)** A cartogram that distorts the geographic geometry of countries by population size.
- **(D)** A choropleth map that shades political subdivisions based on predefined data ranges.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Summary Rationale:** Graduated (or proportional) symbol maps scale the physical area or diameter of a symbol (such as a circle) in direct proportion to the numerical value of the data point being mapped.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** Isoline maps connect points of equal value (e.g., elevation contours or weather isotherms) with continuous lines.
  - **(C):** Cartograms distort and resize geographic land boundaries based on statistical attributes, rather than placing proportional circles on a real base map.
  - **(D):** Choropleth maps apply tonal shading or colors inside established administrative units (like counties or states).
</details>

### Question 2 (1.1-Q02)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 1.B` — *Explain geographic concepts, processes, models, and theories.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Map Projections and Distortion (SADD) (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 1.1, under 'Map Projections and Distortion'*)

> **Prompt:** Which of the following statements best explains why it is geometrically impossible to display Earth on a two-dimensional flat sheet without introducing distortion?

- **(A)** Tectonic continental drift alters global coordinates faster than cartographic projection formulas can recalculate them.
- **(B)** Flattening a three-dimensional curved spheroid onto a two-dimensional planar sheet inherently stretches, tears, or compresses space.
- **(C)** Satellite sensors capture spatial data through curved radial optical lenses that systematically skew coordinate intervals.
- **(D)** Cartographers must omit elevation contour data whenever projecting spherical sovereign borders onto flat rectangular paper grids.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(B)**
- **Summary Rationale:** Every flat map introduces distortion because a three-dimensional sphere cannot be projected onto a two-dimensional flat surface without tearing, stretching, or compressing at least one of the four spatial properties: Shape, Area, Distance, or Direction (SADD).
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(A):** Plate tectonics operates over millions of years at centimeters per year and has no bearing on 2D map projection geometry.
  - **(C):** Satellite lens distortion is mathematically corrected during orthorectification and is not the root mathematical cause of map distortion.
  - **(D):** Elevation can be displayed on 2D maps via isolines or hillshading; its inclusion or omission does not cause projection distortion.
</details>

### Question 6 (1.1-Q06)

- **AMSCO Reference:** Mercator Projection and Navigation (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 1.1, under 'Mercator Navigation Advantage'*)

> **Prompt:** Why did transoceanic navigators in the sixteenth through twentieth centuries rely heavily on the Mercator projection despite its dramatic landmass distortions?

- **(A)** It accurately displayed the relative land area of equatorial states without distortion.
- **(B)** Lines of constant compass direction plot as straight lines, enabling simple navigation bearings.
- **(C)** It maintained true physical mileage distances across the vast expanse of the Pacific Ocean.
- **(D)** It balanced all four geometric properties by mathematically averaging continental errors.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(B)**
- **Summary Rationale:** On a Mercator projection, lines of constant compass direction (rhumb lines or loxodromes) appear as straight lines. A mariner can draw a straight line between two ports and follow that exact compass bearing without recalculating headings.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(A):** Mercator preserves equatorial shape well, but its main navigation advantage was direction, not land area preservation.
  - **(C):** Distance on a Mercator projection is distorted and varies continuously with latitude, making distance measurements complex.
  - **(D):** Averaging errors to create a balanced view describes compromise projections like the Robinson, not the conformal Mercator.
</details>
