### Question 1 (1.6-Q01)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 1.A` — *Describe geographic concepts, processes, models, and theories.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Cartographic Scale vs. Scale of Analysis (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 1.6, under 'Understanding Scale: Cartographic vs. Geographic'*)

> **Prompt:** Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes cartographic scale from a scale of analysis in human geography?

- **(A)** Cartographic scale is the mathematical map-to-ground ratio, while scale of analysis is the level of data aggregation.
- **(B)** Cartographic scale measures qualitative cultural values, while scale of analysis measures exact satellite altitudes.
- **(C)** Cartographic scale represents global trade pacts, while scale of analysis calculates polar azimuthal coordinate grids.
- **(D)** Cartographic scale determines legislative redistricting, while scale of analysis dictates sovereign nautical limits.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Summary Rationale:** Cartographic scale refers to the mathematical ratio between distance on a map and distance on Earth (e.g., 1:24,000), whereas scale of analysis refers to the geographic level at which spatial data is aggregated and analyzed (global, regional, national, subnational, or local).
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** Cartographic scale is mathematical, not qualitative; scale of analysis organizes data boundaries, not satellite heights.
  - **(C):** Cartographic scale is a map ratio, not a treaty; scale of analysis is an analytical boundary, not an azimuthal chart.
  - **(D):** Redistricting is a political boundary process, and nautical limits are defined by maritime law, not cartographic scale.
</details>

  - **(C):** Subnational scale would display individual provinces or states (like California, Bavaria, or Guangdong).

- **AMSCO Reference:** The Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP) (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 1.6, under 'The Modifiable Areal Unit Problem'*)

> **Prompt:** Which of the following scenarios best illustrates the Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP) in geographic data analysis?

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**

- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** High-latitude land distortion on a Mercator map is cartographic projection distortion, not MAUP.
  - **(C):** Applying group aggregate statistics to an individual is the ecological fallacy, not MAUP.
  - **(D):** Air conditioning in desert cities is environmental possibilism, not statistical boundary modifiability.
</details>
