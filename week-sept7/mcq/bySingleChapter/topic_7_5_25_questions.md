### Question 1 (7.5-Q01)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 1.A` — *Describe geographic concepts, processes, models, and theories.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Rostow's Stages: Stage 1 Traditional Society (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 7.5, under 'Rostow's Stages of Economic Growth: Stage 1'*)

> **Prompt:** According to W.W. Rostow's Stages of Economic Growth model, what fundamental condition defines Stage 1: The Traditional Society?

- **(A)** High employment in subsistence agriculture, limited technological capability, and local barter trade.
- **(B)** Rapid expansion in automated robotics manufacturing, venture capital banking, and global trade.
- **(C)** Widespread consumer purchasing of durable household electronics, private automobiles, and luxury goods.
- **(D)** Universal tertiary and quaternary white-collar employment across corporate financial headquarters.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Summary Rationale:** Rostow's Stage 1 (Traditional Society) is characterized by a high percentage of the workforce engaged in subsistence agriculture, static social structures, low productivity, and resistance to technological innovation, with commerce restricted to local barter.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** Industrial automation and venture capital emerge in Stage 4 (Drive to Maturity) and Stage 5 (High Mass Consumption).
  - **(C):** Mass consumer durable goods purchases define Stage 5 (Age of High Mass Consumption).
  - **(D):** Tertiary and quaternary corporate predominance characterizes post-industrial Stage 5 economies.
</details>

- **CED Skill:** `Skill 2.C` — *Explain a possible cause or effect of a spatial pattern or relationship.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Ecotourism and Sustainable Development (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 7.5, under 'Sustainable Development: Ecotourism'*)

> **Prompt:** How does well-managed ecotourism contribute to sustainable economic development in biodiversity-rich countries like Costa Rica?

- **(A)** It generates local jobs and revenue while providing financial incentives to preserve fragile ecosystems.
- **(B)** It clears tropical rainforest canopies to construct massive high-rise casino resorts and cruise docks.
- **(C)** It mandates that local indigenous residents abandon all cultural traditions in favor of factory labor.
- **(D)** It replaces all domestic currency circulation with foreign speculative cryptocurrency investments.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Summary Rationale:** Ecotourism is responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment, sustains local human well-being, and involves interpretation and education. It provides economic alternatives to logging or poaching by generating revenue from guided nature tours, eco-lodges, and park fees.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** Clearing rainforests for high-rise mega-resorts is mass tourism, which destroys the ecological base ecotourism seeks to protect.
  - **(C):** Ecotourism actively celebrates and integrates local indigenous culture and traditional ecological knowledge.
  - **(D):** Ecotourism injects conventional foreign tourist revenues into local businesses rather than speculative crypto assets.
</details>

> **Prompt:** What core philosophical principle underpins the United Nations 2030 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)?

- **(A)** Economic expansion should prioritize fossil fuel extraction regardless of future carbon consequences.
- **(B)** Human development must integrate economic growth with poverty relief, gender equity, and planetary health.
- **(C)** All peripheral developing nations must achieve complete economic autarky by ending international trade.
- **(D)** Technological patents should be restricted exclusively to affluent post-industrial core member states.

- **Why other options are incorrect:**

### Question 23 (7.5-Q23)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 2.C` — *Explain a possible cause or effect of a spatial pattern or relationship.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Upward Mobility in World Systems: The Asian Tigers (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 7.5, under 'Case Studies: Asian Tigers and Upward Mobility'*)

> **Prompt:** How does the post-1960 economic rise of South Korea provide an empirical challenge to the static assumptions of Dependency Theory?

- **(A)** It demonstrates that peripheral nations can achieve wealth only by conquering overseas colonial empires.
- **(B)** It proves that agricultural subsistence barter is the single fastest mechanism to attain global wealth.
- **(C)** It shows that targeted state policies and education can propel a peripheral nation into the semi-periphery and core.
- **(D)** It confirms that foreign direct investment permanently traps developing states in primary copper mining.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(C)**
- **Summary Rationale:** South Korea was a war-torn, impoverished peripheral nation in the 1950s. Through state-led industrial policy, heavy investment in education, export manufacturing, and technological upgrading (chaebols), it ascended into the semi-periphery and is today a full core nation, proving that peripheral status is not an inescapable permanent trap.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(A):** South Korea achieved development through domestic industrialization and global trade, not military colonial conquests.
  - **(B):** South Korea shifted rapidly from subsistence agriculture to advanced electronics, autos, and semiconductors.
  - **(D):** South Korea's ascension directly refutes the claim that peripheral states are perpetually trapped in raw material extraction.
</details>

### Question 24 (7.5-Q24)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 1.B` — *Explain geographic concepts, processes, models, and theories.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** The Circular Economy versus Linear Models (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 7.5, under 'Sustainable Development: The Circular Economy'*)

> **Prompt:** In contemporary sustainable economic geography, how does a circular economy model contrast with the traditional linear industrial model?

- **(A)** It maximizes immediate landfill waste disposal by manufacturing single-use disposable consumer plastics.
- **(B)** It eliminates all international maritime container shipping in favor of transcontinental trucking fleets.
- **(C)** It mandates that every commercial industrial enterprise relocate into offshore tax-exempt free trade zones.
- **(D)** It designs out waste through material reuse, refurbishment, and closed-loop recycling across production cycles.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(D)**
- **Summary Rationale:** The traditional industrial economy is linear ('take-make-waste'). In contrast, a circular economy is regenerative by design: products are designed for durability, disassembly, repair, and closed-loop recycling, keeping materials in productive economic use indefinitely and drastically reducing virgin resource extraction.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(A):** Single-use plastics and landfill waste embody the linear model that circular economies actively eliminate.
  - **(B):** Circular principles apply to manufacturing design and resource recycling, not abolishing maritime freight.
  - **(C):** Tax-exempt free trade zones relate to tariff policy, not circular material recovery engineering.
</details>

### Question 25 (7.5-Q25)
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 5.B` — *Explain spatial relationships across different scales of analysis.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Synthesizing Modernization and World Systems Theories (*AMSCO 2nd Ed, Topic 7.5, under 'Comparing Development Theories'*)

> **Prompt:** When contrasting Rostow's Modernization Model with Wallerstein's World Systems Theory, what fundamental geographical divide emerges?

- **(A)** Rostow analyzes regional linguistic dialects, whereas Wallerstein examines physical tectonic fault lines.
- **(B)** Rostow rejects all industrial manufacturing, whereas Wallerstein advocates returning to subsistence farming.
- **(C)** Rostow views development as impossible, whereas Wallerstein claims every country will modernize equally.
- **(D)** Rostow views development as an internal national process, whereas Wallerstein views it as a global structure.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(D)**
- **Summary Rationale:** The fundamental divide in development theory: Rostow views development through an internal, national lens where every country can progress sequentially through 5 stages given the right policies; Wallerstein views development through a global structural lens, arguing the core cannot exist without an exploited periphery.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(A):** Neither model is focused on linguistic dialects or physical earthquake faults; both analyze economic development.
  - **(B):** Both theorists study industrial capitalism and manufacturing, not pastoral subsistence farming.
  - **(C):** Rostow is optimistic about universal linear modernization, while Wallerstein emphasizes systemic structural inequality.
</details>
