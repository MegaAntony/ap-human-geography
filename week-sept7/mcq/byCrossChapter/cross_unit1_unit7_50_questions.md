### Question 1 (CROSS-01)
- **Focus Topics:** `Topics 1.6 / 7.3`
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 5.B` — *Explain spatial relationships across various scales of analysis.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Cross-Topic Integration: Scales of Analysis & Measures of Development — *Analyzing Disparities Across Scales* (AMSCO 2nd Ed, compare Topic 1.6 ('How Scale Impacts Conclusions') with Topic 7.3 ('Measures of Development'))

> **Scenario / Stimulus:** A world choropleth map displays Brazil with a relatively high national Gross National Income (GNI) per capita of $15,000. However, a sub-national state-level map reveals that southeastern states like São Paulo exceed $24,000 while northeastern interior states like Maranhão drop below $5,000.

> **Question Prompt:** Which of the following geographic concepts best explains why the two maps convey fundamentally different impressions of Brazilian economic development?

- **(A)** Aggregating data at the national scale conceals profound regional disparities in socioeconomic wealth.
- **(B)** Mercator projection distortion artificially expands the land area of tropical South American states.
- **(C)** Remote sensing satellites fail to collect reliable socioeconomic statistics across interior landscapes.
- **(D)** Southeastern Brazil operates completely within an unrecorded subsistence traditional farming system.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Summary Rationale:** This bridges Topic 1.6 (Scale of Analysis) with Topic 7.3 (Measures of Development): national-scale data aggregates the entire country's output into a single macro-average, concealing severe subnational core-periphery inequalities between wealthy industrial hubs and impoverished rural regions.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(B):** Mercator projection inflates high-latitude polar areas while keeping equatorial zones relatively compact, but cartographic distortion does not change statistical wealth values.
  - **(C):** Socioeconomic metrics like GNI per capita are collected through government censuses and tax registries, not via orbital optical satellite sensors.
  - **(D):** Southeastern Brazil, anchored by São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, is a modern industrial and financial core, not a subsistence agrarian society.
</details>

### Question 2 (CROSS-02)
- **Focus Topics:** `Topics 1.1 / 7.5`
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 4.F` — *Explain possible limitations of the visual source or data provided.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Cross-Topic Integration: Map Projections & World Systems Theory — *Cartographic Bias in Global Economic Models* (AMSCO 2nd Ed, compare Topic 1.1 ('Mercator vs. Peters') with Topic 7.5 ('Wallerstein's World Systems'))

> **Scenario / Stimulus:** A geographer criticizes standard classroom cylindrical projection maps for reinforcing Eurocentric geopolitical attitudes by depicting the European and North American Core as vastly larger in physical land area than the tropical African and South American Periphery.

> **Question Prompt:** Which map projection is the subject of this critique, and what mathematical property causes this distortion?

- **(A)** The Gall-Peters projection, which severely compresses equatorial developing nations along latitude lines.
- **(B)** The Mercator projection, which preserves local navigational bearings but heavily magnifies polar areas.
- **(C)** The Robinson projection, which completely eliminates every visual element of distortion on world maps.
- **(D)** The polar azimuthal projection, which accurately depicts tropical landforms while distorting the poles.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(B)**
- **Summary Rationale:** This integrates Topic 1.1 (Map Projections) with Topic 7.5 (World Systems Theory): the conformal Mercator projection inflates land areas near the poles where core countries (North America, Europe) are situated, while visually minimizing equatorial continents where peripheral nations reside.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(A):** The Gall-Peters projection is an equal-area map created specifically to correct Eurocentric bias by accurately preserving relative landmass areas.
  - **(C):** The Robinson projection is a compromise projection that balances distortion of shape, size, and distance, but it cannot eliminate distortion entirely.
  - **(D):** Polar azimuthal projections center on the poles and severely distort distance and shape as one moves outward toward the equator.
</details>

### Question 3 (CROSS-03)
- **Focus Topics:** `Topics 1.4 / 7.2`
- **CED Skill:** `Skill 2.C` — *Explain a likely outcome in a geographic scenario using concepts.*
- **AMSCO Reference:** Cross-Topic Integration: Distance Decay & Weber's Theory — *Transportation Friction and Industrial Location* (AMSCO 2nd Ed, compare Topic 1.4 ('Friction of Distance') with Topic 7.2 ('Weber's Least Cost Theory'))

> **Scenario / Stimulus:** A commercial bottling enterprise manufactures carbonated beverages by mixing concentrated syrup with locally sourced municipal water and packaging the finished drinks into heavy glass bottles for distribution across a metropolitan region.

> **Question Prompt:** Applying Alfred Weber's Least Cost Theory alongside the friction of distance, where should this bottling company locate its processing plant?

- **(A)** Adjacent to distant rural agricultural fields where corn sweeteners and concentrated flavorings originate.
- **(B)** At a remote deepwater oceanic break-of-bulk terminal to facilitate intercontinental bulk raw shipments.
- **(C)** Close to urban consumer retail outlets to minimize the expensive transport costs of heavy liquid products.
- **(D)** Equidistant between raw ingredient suppliers and high-latitude peripheral commodity extraction enclaves.

<details><summary><b>Reveal Answer & Detailed Rationale</b></summary>

- **Correct Answer:** **(C)**
- **Summary Rationale:** This connects Topic 1.4 (Friction of Distance) with Topic 7.2 (Weber's Least Cost Theory): beverage bottling adds water and heavy packaging, making the finished product heavier, bulkier, and more expensive to ship than raw syrup. It is bulk-gaining and must locate near the market.
- **Why other options are incorrect:**
  - **(A):** Locating near rural cornfields would maximize shipping expenses for the heavier, water-filled finished product across long distances to urban consumers.
  - **(B):** A break-of-bulk oceanic port is vital for imported raw goods, but carbonated drinks utilize local municipal water and serve immediate domestic markets.
  - **(D):** Weberian least-cost optimization calculates friction of distance based on product weight dynamics, not arbitrary geographic midpoints.
</details>
