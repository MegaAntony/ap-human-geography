Pre-Formatted Career: Biomedical Engineer

Biotechnology Research & Development Pathway • South Forsyth High School

Open Canvas Assignment

Part 1: Profile & Daily Tasks

Role: Applies engineering, biology, and medical principles to design and test life-saving devices, prosthetics, and surgical robots.

5 Tasks: 1) Surgeon clinical consults; 2) 3D CAD modeling (SolidWorks); 3) Biocompatibility lab testing; 4) Zero-tolerance machine calibration; 5) FDA risk documentation.

Setting: Research cleanrooms, computing labs, and hospital surgical suites. High accountability for human life.

Part 2: Education & Teamwork

Degree: 4-Year B.S. in Biomedical Engineering (Georgia Tech top program); M.S./Ph.D. for research directors.

Licensing: Fundamentals of Engineering (FE/EIT) exam followed by Georgia PE license.

Teamwork: Collaborates with surgeons, nurses, IT specialists, and safety officers to ensure seamless clinical use.

Part 3: Pros, Cons & Surprises

Pros: Massive patient impact (saving thousands of lives at once); exciting work with AI & bionics; high salaries ($65k start ➔ $162k senior).

Cons: Demanding college math/science curriculum; 3–7 year FDA clinical trial wait times; high liability.

Surprise: Engineers scrub directly into hospital operating rooms to watch surgeons test devices in real time.

Part 4: Antony's 3 High School Steps

Step 1: Complete SFHS Healthcare Science pathway + AP Biology, AP Chemistry, and AP Calculus.

Step 2: Compete in SFHS HOSA (Medical Innovation, Biomedical Debate) leveraging international 1st place distinction.

Step 3: 50+ hours of hospital volunteering and clinical shadowing at Northside Hospital Forsyth or Emory Johns Creek.

Career Profile & Description

Biotechnology Pathway
Mission Statement

Applies engineering principles and medical science to design, build, and test life-saving devices, diagnostic equipment, and artificial organs that improve patient recovery and quality of life.

Key Specializations
  • Bionic prosthetics and neural interfaces
  • Robotic-assisted surgical systems (e.g. DaVinci)
  • Cardiovascular pacemakers & stent design
  • 3D biocompatible tissue scaffolds

A Typical Day (5 Responsibilities)

Mandatory Topic
1
Surgical Collaboration

Consults directly with surgeons in hospitals to understand clinical requirements.

2
3D CAD Modeling

Designs prosthetic joints and device prototypes using SolidWorks.

3
Biocompatibility Lab Tests

Performs stress and immune tests to guarantee tissue compatibility.

4
Hardware Calibration

Calibrates hospital MRI machines, CT scanners, and surgical arms.

5
FDA Risk Analysis & Documentation

Drafts clinical trial reports and Design History Files for federal safety approvals.

Education, Credentials & Salary

BLS 2026 Data
Degree & Licensing
  • Minimum Degree: Bachelor of Science (B.S.) in Biomedical Engineering (4 yrs).
  • Top GA School: Georgia Tech (ranked #1/2 nationally).
  • Licensure in GA: FE/EIT Exam ➔ Georgia Professional Engineer (PE) License.
Earning Comparison
Starting Salary (Entry): $65,400 – $78,200
Experienced (Senior/Lead): $135,800 – $162,500+

Projected 10-year job growth: 10% (faster than average).

Workplace Environment & Team Culture

Settings

Cleanrooms, biomedical device R&D labs, and hospital surgical suites.

Stress Level

High accountability. Stress stems from zero-error tolerances and strict FDA timelines.

Healthcare Team

Surgeons, surgical techs, IT specialists, nurses, and FDA compliance auditors.

Benefits & Challenges (3 Pros & 3 Cons)

PROS (Benefits)
  • ✔ Massive Patient Impact: One single innovation can help hundreds of thousands of patients worldwide.
  • ✔ High-Tech Frontier: Daily hands-on work with surgical robotics, bionics, and AI medicine.
  • ✔ Strong Job Security & Pay: High starting wages with rapid industry expansion in Georgia.
CONS (Challenges)
  • ✖ Demanding Degree: Grueling coursework in advanced calculus, physics, and organic chemistry.
  • ✖ Lengthy FDA Timelines: Medical device approvals can take 3 to 7 years of clinical trials.
  • ✖ High Liability: Zero tolerance for product defects when human life depends on your device.
