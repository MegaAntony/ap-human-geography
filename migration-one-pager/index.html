Migration One Pager: Complete Visual Blueprint & Requirements

A "One Pager" is a single-page visual synthesis of a major unit topic. It combines geographic vocabulary, central quotes, push/pull factor diagrams, Ravenstein's laws, map migration corridors, and real-world case studies on an unlined sheet of paper.

The 6 Mandatory One-Pager Zones

Organize your paper into these 6 structural sections for full credit

Zone 1 • Center

Central Symbolic Image & Title

A large, central illustration that represents your chosen migration topic (e.g., luggage with passport stamps, a migrant border path, or a divided globe). A prominent title with essential thematic typography.

Tip: Must be colored and hand-drawn (no white space left unfilled).
Zone 2 • Theory

Ravenstein's Laws of Migration

Include at least 3 of E.G. Ravenstein's laws illustrated with icons:

  • Most migrants travel short distances (Distance Decay).
  • Migrants traveling long distances head to major urban centers.
  • Rural residents are more likely to migrate than urban residents.
  • Adults migrate more than families with children.
Key Term: Gravity Model & Distance Decay
Zone 3 • Drivers

Push vs. Pull Factors T-Chart

Clear visual comparison of conditions inducing migration across 4 categories:

• Economic: Poverty/unemployment (push) vs. Jobs/high wages (pull).

• Social/Cultural: Religious persecution vs. Freedom.

• Political: War, tyranny vs. Political stability.

• Environmental: Drought, famine, flooding vs. Safe climate.

Include: Intervening Obstacles vs. Opportunities
Zone 4 • Vocab

Essential Vocabulary Corner

Integrate at least 5 core terms with your own concise definitions:

• Refugee: Forced to migrate across international borders due to armed conflict/persecution.

• IDP (Internally Displaced Person): Forced to flee like a refugee but has not crossed an international boundary.

• Chain Migration: Migration of people to a location because relatives or members of same nationality previously migrated there.

• Step Migration: Migration in stages (e.g., farm → village → small town → big city).

• Transhumance: Seasonal migration of pastoralists.

Highlight: Distinction between Refugee vs. IDP vs. Asylum Seeker
Zone 5 • Case Study

Real-World Migration Corridor

Illustrate a historical or contemporary migration flow on a sketch map:

  • Syrian Civil War: Forced refugee stream to Turkey & Europe.
  • Venezuela Exodus: Economic hyperinflation to Colombia/US.
  • Great Migration (US 1916–1970): African Americans leaving rural South for Northern industrial cities.
  • Guest Workers (Gulf States): South Asians moving to UAE/Qatar for construction.
Illustrate: Origin, Destination & Flow Arrow
Zone 6 • Impacts

Consequences: Origin vs. Destination

Show the two-way impacts of migration:

• Impact on Origin: Remittances (financial transfers sent back), loss of working-age population, Brain Drain (loss of educated professionals).

• Impact on Destination: Low-cost labor, cultural diversification, potential anti-immigrant sentiment/xenophobia, population stabilization in aging societies.

Essential Concept: Remittance dependency & Brain Drain
