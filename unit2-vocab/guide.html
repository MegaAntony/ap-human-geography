Cluster 1: Population Density & Spatial Distribution

Topics 2.1 – 2.3 • Terms 1 through 8

Arithmetic Density
Total People ÷ Total Land

Crude population count per square mile or kilometer. Measures broad density, but hides internal clusters (e.g., US average is 36/km², but NYC is 28,000/km² and Alaska is <1/km²).

Physiological Density
Total People ÷ Arable Land

Measures food carrying capacity pressure. Shows how many mouths each piece of farmable land must feed. Classic Exam Example: Egypt's arithmetic is 103, but physiological is 3,500+ because 97% of Egypt is desert!

Agricultural Density
Farmers ÷ Arable Land

Reflects economic development and technology. MDCs (US, Canada) have very low agricultural density because combines and tractors do the work. LDCs (Uganda, Bangladesh) have high density (lots of peasant hand laborers).

Carrying Capacity & Overpopulation

Carrying capacity is the maximum population an ecosystem can sustain indefinitely. Overpopulation occurs when people exceed resources—it is NOT just high population! Singapore has 6M people on a tiny island but is wealthy; an arid Sahel country with 2M can be dangerously overpopulated if it cannot feed or water its citizens.

The Ecumene & The 4 World Clusters

Ecumene = permanently settled Earth. 2/3 of humans live in 4 regions: East Asia (China coast/rivers), South Asia (Indus/Ganges), Southeast Asia (volcanic islands like Java), and Europe (industrial coalfields). Non-ecumene zones: polar ice, hyper-arid deserts, dense rainforests, high alpine peaks.

Cluster 2: Demographic Rates & Mathematical Formulas

Topics 2.4 – 2.6 • Terms 9 through 20

🧮 The 3 Mathematical Formulas Mrs. Knight Tests:

Natural Increase Rate (NIR)
(CBR - CDR) ÷ 10
Converts rate per 1,000 into a %
Doubling Time (Rule of 70)
70 ÷ NIR (%)
Number of years to double population
Demographic Balancing Eq.
(B - D) + (I - E)
Natural change + Net migration
Total Fertility Rate (TFR)

The average number of children a woman will have throughout her childbearing years (15–49). Replacement Level is 2.1 (2 replaces both parents, 0.1 accounts for infant mortality). Sub-replacement (<2.1) leads to shrinking populations without migration.

Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)

Number of infant deaths (<1 year old) per 1,000 live births. Demographers consider IMR the single most accurate indicator of a nation's overall healthcare, female nutrition, and sanitation systems.

CBR vs. CDR Trap

Highly developed nations with large elderly populations (e.g., Germany, Japan) often have a higher CDR (~11–12) than developing nations with young populations (e.g., Guatemala or Saudi Arabia, CDR ~3–5)!

Cluster 3: Age Structures & Population Pyramids

Topics 2.7 – 2.8 • Terms 21 through 26

Stage 2: Wide Base Pyramid
Rapid Growth (e.g., Nigeria, Mali)

Broad triangle base indicating high CBR. High youth dependency ratio putting massive fiscal strain on pediatric clinics, schools, and immunization systems.

Stage 4: Rectangular Column
Stable / ZPG (e.g., USA, France)

Even bars across cohorts from youth to adulthood. TFR ~2.1. Balanced working-age tax base supporting stable social security and healthcare systems.

Stage 5: Inverted / Constricted
Negative Growth (e.g., Japan, Italy)

Narrow base, bulging top. TFR < 1.5. Extreme elderly dependency ratio causing severe labor shortages and bankrupting pension systems.

Dependency Ratio & Sex Ratio Distortions:

Dependency Ratio Formula: $[(Pop\ 0\text{--}14 + Pop\ 65+) \div Pop\ 15\text{--}64] \times 100$. Strains are either youth-based (schools/vaccines) or elderly-based (pensions/geriatrics).
Sex Ratio: Males per 100 females. Biological standard is ~105:100. Heavily distorted in the Persian Gulf (over 200:100 due to foreign male construction guest workers) and in China/India (historical son preference / One-Child policy).

Cluster 4: Demographic & Epidemiological Models

Topics 2.9 – 2.10 • Terms 27 through 41

Stage Birth Rate (CBR) Death Rate (CDR) NIR Historical Catalyst & Diseases (ETM) Examples
Stage 1 High High ~0% Pestilence & Famine (Black Plague, starvation, animal attacks). No countries today
Stage 2 High Plummets Exploding Industrial Rev (1800s West) / Medical Rev (1950s LDCs: antibiotics, DDT). Receding pandemics. Mali, Niger, Yemen
Stage 3 Plummets Low Slows Urbanization, female schooling, contraception. Degenerative diseases (heart, cancer). Mexico, Brazil, India
Stage 4 Low Low ~0% (ZPG) Delayed degenerative diseases (advanced medical bypasses, stents, chemo). USA, France, UK

Thomas Malthus (1798)

Argued population grows geometrically (exponentially: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16) while food grows arithmetically (linearly: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Predicted inevitable starvation catastrophe.
Neo-Malthusians say Malthus is right today because population depletes fresh water, clean air, topsoil, and fossil fuels.

Ester Boserup (Anti-Malthusian)

Argued "Necessity is the mother of invention". As population density grows, humans invent new agricultural technologies (irrigation, Green Revolution hybrid seeds, mechanization) that multiply crop yields.

Cluster 5 & 6: Migration Patterns, Laws & Classifications

Topics 2.11 – 2.12 • Terms 42 through 72

🛡️ The 3 Displaced Persons Categories (Guaranteed on the Test!):

1. Refugee

Forced to flee due to war/persecution and has crossed an international border. Legally protected by 1951 UN Convention (non-refoulement).

2. IDP (Internally Displaced)

Fled for the same reasons as a refugee, but remains inside their own country (e.g., Ukrainians moving to Lviv). No UN treaty protection.

3. Asylum Seeker

Crossed a border and formally applied for legal refugee protection; case is actively pending in immigration court.

Ravenstein’s Laws of Migration (1885)
  • Most migrants move short distances (distance decay).
  • Long-distance migrants choose major economic urban centers.
  • Migration occurs in steps (step migration).
  • Rural people are more mobile than urban dwellers.
  • Historically, young single adults move more than families.
  • Economic reasons are the primary driver of migration.
Intervening Obstacle vs. Opportunity

• Intervening Obstacle: A barrier (desert, ocean, border wall, strict visa quota) that blocks or delays migration.
• Intervening Opportunity: A positive feature (a good job opening along the journey) that convinces the migrant to settle early before reaching their planned destination.

Chain Migration: Moving to a neighborhood because kin/relatives moved there earlier $\to$ forms Ethnic Enclaves (Chinatown, Little Havana).

Top 5 Traps to Avoid on Mrs. Knight’s Vocab Summative

These 5 distinctions make the difference between an 85% and a 100%

Trap 1: Physiological vs. Agricultural Density

Physiological = People per arable land (food carrying capacity).
Agricultural = Farmers per arable land (farming technology level).

Trap 2: Refugee vs. IDP

A Refugee crossed an international border (has UN treaty protection).
An IDP stayed inside their own country (no UN treaty protection).

Trap 3: NIR Formula Division

Always divide by 10! $(CBR - CDR) \div 10$. If $CBR = 20$ and $CDR = 5$, $NIR = 1.5\%$, never 15%.

Trap 4: Malthus vs. Boserup

Malthus = Food grows linearly, humans exponentially $\to$ unavoidable famine.
Boserup = Population pressure stimulates agricultural invention (Green Rev).

Trap 5: Why Stage 2 Death Rate Plunged

In Western Europe/US: **Industrial Revolution** (1800s steam engines, sanitation, wealth).
In Developing World (Africa, Asia, Latin America): **Medical Revolution** (1950s imported antibiotics, vaccines, DDT).
