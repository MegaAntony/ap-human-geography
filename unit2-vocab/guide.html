01

Cluster 1: Population Density & Spatial Distribution

Arithmetic Density
Total People ÷ Total Land

Crude population count per square mile or kilometer. Measures broad density, but hides internal clusters (e.g., US average is 36/km², but NYC is 28,000/km² and Alaska is <1/km²).

Physiological Density
Total People ÷ Arable Land

Measures food carrying capacity pressure. Shows how many mouths each piece of farmable land must feed. Classic Exam Example: Egypt's arithmetic is 103, but physiological is 3,500+ because 97% of Egypt is desert!

Agricultural Density
Farmers ÷ Arable Land

Reflects economic development and technology. MDCs (US, Canada) have low agricultural density because combines and tractors do the work. LDCs (Uganda, Bangladesh) have high density (lots of hand laborers).

Carrying Capacity & Overpopulation

Carrying capacity is the maximum population an ecosystem can sustain. Overpopulation occurs when people exceed resources—it is NOT just high population! Singapore has 6M people in a tiny city but is wealthy; an arid Sahel country with 2M can be dangerously overpopulated.

The Ecumene & The 4 World Clusters

Ecumene = permanently settled Earth. 2/3 of humans live in 4 regions: East Asia (China coast/rivers), South Asia (Indus/Ganges), Southeast Asia (volcanic islands), and Europe (industrial coalfields).

Cluster 2: Demographic Rates & Mathematical Formulas

🧮 The 3 Mathematical Formulas Mrs. Knight Tests:

Natural Increase Rate (NIR)
(CBR - CDR) ÷ 10
Gives annual growth as a %
Doubling Time (Rule of 70)
70 ÷ NIR (%)
Years to double population
Balancing Equation
(B - D) + (I - E)
Natural change + Net migration
Total Fertility Rate (TFR) & Replacement Level

The average number of children a woman will have in childbearing years (15–49). Replacement Level is 2.1 (2 replaces parents, 0.1 accounts for infant mortality). Sub-replacement (<2.1) leads to shrinking populations without migration.

Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)

Number of infant deaths (<1 year old) per 1,000 live births. It is widely considered by demographers to be the single most accurate indicator of a nation's overall healthcare, sanitation, and female nutrition.

03

Cluster 3: Age Structures & Population Pyramids

Stage 2: Wide Base Pyramid
Rapid Growth (e.g., Nigeria, Mali)

Broad triangle base indicating high CBR. High youth dependency ratio putting massive fiscal strain on pediatric clinics, schools, and immunization systems.

Stage 4: Rectangular Column
Stable / ZPG (e.g., USA, France)

Even bars across cohorts from youth to adulthood. TFR ~2.1. Balanced working-age tax base supporting stable social security systems.

Stage 5: Inverted / Constricted
Negative Growth (e.g., Japan, Italy)

Narrow base, bulging top. TFR < 1.5. Extreme elderly dependency ratio causing severe labor shortages and bankrupting pension systems.

Dependency Ratio & Sex Ratio Distortions:

Dependency Ratio: $[(Pop\ 0\text{--}14 + Pop\ 65+) \div Pop\ 15\text{--}64] \times 100$. Strains are either youth-based or elderly-based.
Sex Ratio: Males per 100 females. Skewed heavily male in the Persian Gulf (foreign male construction guest workers) and in China/India (historical son preference / One-Child policy).

Cluster 4: Demographic & Epidemiological Models

Stage Birth Rate Death Rate NIR Historical Catalyst & Diseases
Stage 1 High High ~0% Pestilence & Famine (Black Plague, hunger). No countries today.
Stage 2 High Plummets Exploding Industrial & Medical Revolutions. Receding pandemics (cholera).
Stage 3 Plummets Low Slows Urbanization, female schooling, contraception. Degenerative diseases (heart, cancer).
Stage 4 Low Low ~0% (ZPG) Delayed degenerative diseases (advanced medical bypasses, stents).
Stage 5 Very Low Rises above CBR Negative Re-emergence of superbugs / antibiotic resistance; shrinking workforce.
Thomas Malthus (1798)

Argued population grows geometrically (exponentially: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16) while food grows arithmetically (linearly: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Predicted inevitable catastrophe.
Neo-Malthusians say Malthus is right today because population depletes water, clean air, topsoil, and energy.

Ester Boserup (Anti-Malthusian)

Argued "Necessity is the mother of invention". As population density grows, humans invent new technologies (irrigation, Green Revolution hybrid seeds, mechanization) that multiply crop yields.

05

Cluster 5 & 6: Migration Patterns, Laws & Classifications

The 3 Displaced Persons Categories (Guaranteed on the Test!):

1. Refugee

Forced to flee due to war/persecution and has crossed an international border. Protected by UN international law (*non-refoulement*).

2. IDP (Internally Displaced)

Fled for the same reasons as a refugee, but remains inside their own country (e.g., Ukrainians moving to Lviv). No UN treaty protection.

3. Asylum Seeker

Crossed a border and formally applied for legal refugee status; case is pending in immigration court.

Ravenstein’s Laws of Migration (1885)
  • Most migrants move short distances (distance decay).
  • Long-distance migrants choose major economic urban centers.
  • Migration occurs in steps (step migration).
  • Rural people are more mobile than urban dwellers.
  • Historically, young single adults move more than families.
  • Economic reasons are the primary driver of migration.
Intervening Obstacle vs. Opportunity

• Intervening Obstacle: A barrier (desert, border wall, strict visa quota) that blocks or delays migration.
• Intervening Opportunity: A positive feature (a good job opening along the journey) that convinces the migrant to settle early before reaching their planned destination.

Chain Migration: Moving to a neighborhood because kin/relatives moved there earlier $\to$ forms Ethnic Enclaves (Chinatown).
