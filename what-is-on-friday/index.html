⚠️ Top 5 Traps College Board & Mrs. Knight Test

1. The Dependency Ratio Denominator Trap:

Total Dependency Ratio is $\frac{\text{Youth (0–14)} + \text{Elderly (65+)}}{\text{Working Age (15–64)}} \times 100$. The denominator is STRICTLY the working-age population (15–64), NEVER total population!

2. The Elderly Crude Death Rate (CDR) Trap:

France (~9.5 CDR) has a higher death rate than Bangladesh (~5.5 CDR) not because of poor healthcare, but because France has an aged population structure (21%+ over 65 vs. 5%).

3. The North Africa Regional Trap:

College Board groups North Africa with Southwest Asia (Middle East), NEVER with Sub-Saharan Africa, due to shared Arabic language, Islamic faith, and arid desert landscape.

4. The Arithmetic vs. Physiological Density Trap:

A low arithmetic density can hide extreme overcrowding. Egypt's arithmetic density is ~105/km², but its physiological density is over 3,100/km² because 95% of the country is desert and people cluster on the 3% arable strip along the Nile.

5. China's "4-2-1" Trap & Skewed Sex Ratio:

A direct result of the One-Child Policy (1979–2015): 1 adult only child supports 2 parents and 4 grandparents with no siblings to share costs, coupled with a skewed ratio of ~115 boys per 100 girls.
