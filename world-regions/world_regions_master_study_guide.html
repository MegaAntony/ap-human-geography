The 10 Macro-Regions

# Region Name on Map Definition & Distinguishing Characteristics Key AP Anchor Points
1 North America Canada, United States, and Greenland. Anglo-American cultural sphere influenced predominantly by British/French heritage. Do NOT include Mexico here on the AP map (Mexico begins Latin America).
2 Central America Narrow isthmus linking North & South America (Guatemala to Panama). Culturally Latin/Spanish heritage. Physically part of the North American continent, but culturally Latin America.
3 South America Continent south of Panama (Colombia to Chile/Argentina). Dominated by Spanish language and Roman Catholic faith, except Portuguese Brazil.
4 Europe Eurasian landmass west of the Ural Mountains and Black Sea. Highly developed, industrialized, Post-Industrial (DTM Stage 4 & 5).
5 The Russian Federation Massive transcontinental state spanning Eastern Europe and Northern Asia (Siberia). Spans 11 time zones; separates Europe from East Asia.
6 Asia Largest continent, bounded by the Arctic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans. High internal diversity; contains over 60% of the human population.
7 Africa (North Africa) Arid zone north of the Sahara desert, culturally linked to Southwest Asia. Predominantly Arabic-speaking and Islamic.
8 Sub-Saharan Africa The entire African landmass south of the Sahara desert. Distinct cultural, linguistic (Niger-Congo), and demographic realm (high TFR).
9 Oceania Australia, New Zealand, and thousands of Pacific island chains. Encompasses Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia.
10 Antarctica Southern polar continent covered by continental glaciers. Governed by the Antarctic Treaty; no permanent sovereign human settlements.

The 5 Oceans

1. Arctic Ocean: Encircles North Pole; borders Canada, Alaska, Russia, Scandinavia.
2. Atlantic Ocean: S-shaped basin separating Americas from Europe & Africa.
3. Pacific Ocean: Largest/deepest ocean; bordered by "Ring of Fire".
4. Indian Ocean: Located between Africa, Indian Subcontinent, and Australia.
5. Southern Ocean: Encircles Antarctica south of 60°S latitude.

Deep Focus 1: Southeast Asia vs. East Asia (The Vietnam Trap)

🚨 The AP Trap: Do NOT place Vietnam in East Asia!
Even though Vietnamese culture was heavily influenced by Chinese Confucianism, geographers strictly classify Vietnam as Southeast Asia based on its physical location in the Indochinese Peninsula.

The Boundary Line

  • • Physical Boundary: Southern political border of China (Yunnan & Guangxi) and Gulf of Tonkin.
  • • East Asia Stops At: The southern border of China.
  • • Southeast Asia Starts At: The northern borders of Myanmar, Laos, and Vietnam.

Contrast Dimensions

  • • East Asia: Mainland + offshore (Japan, Taiwan). Low birth rates (S. Korea $TFR \approx 0.72$).
  • • Southeast Asia: Mainland Indochina + Maritime Archipelago (17,000+ islands in Indonesia). Chokepoint: Strait of Malacca.

Deep Focus 2: Central Asia vs. South Asia (The Pakistan Trap)

⚠️ The Pakistan Trap: Do NOT be tricked by the "-stan" suffix!
Pakistan is 100% SOUTH ASIA. It lies directly in the Indus River Valley on the Indian Subcontinent south of the Himalayas. Central Asia consists exclusively of the 5 post-Soviet republics.

Deep Focus 3: Sub-Saharan Africa's 4 Quadrants

1. West Africa (Atlantic Bulge)

Anchor: Nigeria (Africa's #1 populous nation, 220M+)

Lies along the coast of the Gulf of Guinea. Historic origin of transatlantic slave trade.

2. Central Africa (Equatorial Core)

Anchor: Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC)

Crosses Equator. Dominated by the Congo Basin tropical rainforest and river network.

3. East Africa (Horn & Rift Valley)

Anchors: Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda

Great Rift Valley, Mount Kilimanjaro, African Great Lakes, and Indian Ocean Swahili coast.

4. Southern Africa (Southern Cone)

Anchor: South Africa (plus Botswana, Namibia, Zimbabwe)

Temperate southern plateau, Kalahari Desert, Cape of Good Hope, rich mineral deposits.

The Top 5 College Board & Mrs. Knight Map Traps

★ 1. The Brazil Language Trap

Brazil is distinguished as its own subregion because its official language is Portuguese, whereas the rest of Latin America speaks Spanish.

★ 2. Middle East & North Africa Dual Identity

Frequently grouped together as MENA or SWANA due to shared Arabic language, Islamic faith, and arid climate despite occupying two distinct continents.

★ 3. The Oceania Island Triad

Micronesia (small islands north), Melanesia (dark islands northeast of Australia), and Polynesia (massive triangle: Hawaii to New Zealand to Easter Island).

★ 4. Scale of Analysis Principle (AMSCO p. 45)

Moving from Map 1 (10 regions) to Map 2 (23 subregions) represents zooming in the scale of analysis, revealing internal variation masked at global scale.

★ 5. Formal vs. Perceptual Regional Boundaries

Formal regions (countries, states) have legally defined, measurable borders. Perceptual / Vernacular regions ("The Middle East", "The South") have fuzzy, debatable transition zones based on cultural perception.
