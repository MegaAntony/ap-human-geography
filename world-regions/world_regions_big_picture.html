📚 Regional Breakdown Reference Guide (AMSCO p. 44)

Region / Feature Classification Key AP Human Geography CED Context
North America Continent United States and Canada. Primarily English-speaking with historical British/French cultural heritage.
Central America Cultural Realm Physically part of North America, but separated culturally because of Spanish language, history, and Roman Catholic influence.
South America Continent Shares Iberian heritage (Spanish/Portuguese) with Central America, forming Latin America.
Europe Continent Extends from the Atlantic Ocean eastward to the Ural Mountains. Dense population and high development index.
The Russian Federation Cultural Realm Spans eastern Europe and northern Asia; grouped separately due to Slavic language, Orthodox/Soviet heritage, and vast transcontinental scale.
Africa (North Africa) Continent Northern Africa is physically part of Africa, but culturally linked to Southwest Asia through Arabic language and Islam.
Sub-Saharan Africa Cultural Realm All African nations located south of the Sahara Desert. Distinct indigenous cultures, languages, and colonial experiences.
Asia Continent World's largest continent by area and population, home to approximately 60% of the world's inhabitants.
Oceania Continent Includes Australia, New Zealand, and thousands of Pacific islands across Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia.
Antarctica Continent Southernmost continent, regulated by international treaty, containing scientific research stations but no permanent indigenous settlements.
Oceans Oceans Arctic Ocean (North Polar), Atlantic Ocean, Pacific Ocean, Indian Ocean, and Southern Ocean (surrounding Antarctica).
AP Exam Essential Takeaway: In AP Human Geography, regions are not strictly physical continents. Geographers construct regions based on shared cultural, linguistic, and historical traits. That is why Central America, Sub-Saharan Africa, and The Russian Federation are highlighted as distinct regional units.
