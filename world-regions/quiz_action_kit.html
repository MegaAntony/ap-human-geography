⚠️ Top 4 Boundary Traps Tested Tomorrow

🚨 TRAP 1: The Vietnam Trap (Southeast Asia vs. East Asia)
The Rule: East Asia stops at the southern border of China.
Vietnam is strictly SOUTHEAST ASIA: Even though Vietnam has historical Confucian cultural ties, geographically and on the College Board map, it is part of Mainland Southeast Asia (Indochina).
East Asia: China, Japan, North Korea, South Korea, Taiwan, Mongolia.
Southeast Asia: Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Myanmar, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Philippines, Brunei, Timor-Leste.
🚨 TRAP 2: The Pakistan Trap (South Asia vs. Central Asia)
The Barrier: The Himalayan Mountain Range & Hindu Kush physically wall off South Asia.
Pakistan is 100% SOUTH ASIA: Do not be tricked by the "-stan" suffix! Pakistan lies directly in the Indus River Valley on the Indian Subcontinent.
Central Asia: Strictly the 5 post-Soviet republics (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan).
South Asia: India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Maldives.
🚨 TRAP 3: Sub-Saharan Africa’s 4 Quadrants
Know the anchor nations for each quadrant on Mrs. Knight’s test:
  • West Africa: Atlantic bulge on the Gulf of Guinea (Anchor: Nigeria — Africa's most populous nation).
  • Central Africa: Equatorial heartland in the Congo Basin rainforest (Anchor: Democratic Republic of the Congo).
  • East Africa: Horn of Africa, Great Rift Valley, and African Great Lakes (Anchors: Kenya, Ethiopia, Tanzania).
  • Southern Africa: Southern tip ending at the Cape of Good Hope (Anchor: South Africa).
🚨 TRAP 4: The Brazil Enclave in Latin America
On Map 2 (A Closer Look), Brazil is partitioned as its own distinct subregion separate from the rest of South/Latin America because its official language and colonial heritage is Portuguese, while the surrounding countries are Spanish-speaking.

📚 Key Concepts (AMSCO p. 45 & Fiveable CED)

Scale of Analysis (AMSCO p. 45)
Shifting from Map 1 (10 macro-regions) to Map 2 (23 subregions) zooms in the scale of analysis. Zooming in reveals internal cultural, linguistic, and economic variations that broad continental generalizations completely obscure.
Region Type Defining Characteristic Textbook Examples
Formal Region
(Uniform)		 Defined by a common, measurable shared trait (cultural, political, or physical) across the entire boundary. State of Georgia, Corn Belt, French-speaking Quebec, Sahara Desert.
Functional Region
(Nodal)		 Organized around a central focal node with connections and flows decaying over distance (distance decay). Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta airport flight network, pizza delivery radius, TV broadcast area.
Perceptual Region
(Vernacular)		 Based on informal cultural feelings and subjective personal identities rather than rigid objective borders. "The Middle East", "The American South", "The Rust Belt", "Dixie".
