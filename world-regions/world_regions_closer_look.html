Red Labels: Official AP Subregions & Distinct Cultural Enclaves

📚 Official AP Subregions Breakdown (AMSCO p. 45)

Subregion Parent Region Distinguishing AP CED Traits
Canada & United States North America Separated along the 49th parallel; shared Anglo-American heritage and high urbanization.
Latin America The Americas Overarching cultural realm stretching from Mexico through Tierra del Fuego.
Caribbean Latin America Island subregion with strong African, Spanish, British, and French colonial synthesis.
Brazil Latin America Highlighted as a distinct subregion because its primary language is Portuguese rather than Spanish.
Western Europe & Eastern Europe Europe Historically divided by the Cold War Iron Curtain; distinct linguistic (Romance/Germanic vs Slavic) and economic patterns.
Russia Eurasia Spans 11 time zones across Northern Asia, maintaining dominant cultural and economic influence.
Central Asia Asia Former Soviet republics ("The Stans"); landlocked, arid/steppe geography, Turkic languages, and Silk Road heritage.
Middle East (Southwest Asia) Asia / North Africa Crossroads of continents; Arabic language dominance, birthplace of Abrahamic religions, significant petroleum reserves.
South Asia Asia Indian subcontinent separated by Himalayas; major population cluster, Hinduism/Islam, Hindi/Urdu.
East Asia Asia China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan; Confucian traditions, massive population agglomeration, economic powerhouse.
Southeast Asia Asia Mainland Indochina & maritime archipelago (Indonesia, Philippines); cross-cultural crossroads of Indian and Chinese influences.
North Africa Africa Sahara desert region; linguistically Arabic and predominantly Islamic.
West, Central, East, & Southern Africa Sub-Saharan Africa 4 subregions categorized by geographical location and diverse ethnic/linguistic groupings:
• West Africa: High population density along Gulf of Guinea.
• Central Africa: Congo Basin tropical rainforest.
• East Africa: Great Rift Valley highlands and Swahili coast.
• Southern Africa: Mineral wealth, temperate southern plateau.
Australia, Polynesia, Micronesia, Melanesia Oceania Australia (mainland continent) plus the three major island subregions of the Pacific Ocean.
Key Concept — Scale of Analysis: Notice how changing from "A Big Picture View" to "A Closer Look" represents an increase in map resolution / scale of analysis. Areas that appeared uniform (like Sub-Saharan Africa or Latin America) reveal critical internal diversity when examined up close.
