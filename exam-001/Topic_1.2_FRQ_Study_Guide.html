📊 Section 1: Quantitative vs. Qualitative Geographic Data (Student pp. 18–20 / Key p. 2)
TOPIC 1.2 • QUANTITATIVE DATA Define
📖 Student Book: Pages 18–19 (Sources of Quantitative Data) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Define quantitative geographic data and provide ONE example used in human geography.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Quantitative data consists of numerical information that can be measured, counted, and statistically recorded.

Example: National census population figures, median household income, fertility rates, or agricultural yield statistics."
💡 Memory Hook: Quantitative = Quantity (Math, numbers, tables, graphs).
TOPIC 1.2 • QUALITATIVE DATA Define
📖 Student Book: Page 20 (Qualitative Data: Photo of Tokyo) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Define qualitative geographic data and provide ONE example used in human geography.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Qualitative data consists of descriptive, non-numerical information reflecting human perceptions, opinions, and personal narratives.

Example: Transcripts of resident interviews, personal travel diaries, field sketch notes, or photographs of architectural landscapes."
💡 Memory Hook: Qualitative = Quality & Quotes (Words, photos, feelings, stories).
STUDENT BOOK IN-TEXT FIGURE Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 20 (Photo of Tokyo Visual) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Explain the advantage of using qualitative visual sources (such as photographs or landscape images) compared to quantitative data.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Qualitative visual sources provide firsthand visual context, local perspectives, and cultural insights about the built environment and atmosphere of a place that raw numerical statistics cannot capture."
💡 Memory Hook: A photo of Tokyo shows neon signs, crowding, and culture; numbers only show population counts!
📈 Section 2: Strengths & Weaknesses of Data Representations (Student p. 19 / Key p. 2)
TOPIC 1.2 • DATA FORMATS COMPARISON Compare
📖 Student Book: Page 19 (Sources of Quantitative Data Chart) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Compare the strengths and weaknesses of Maps, Graphs, Data Tables, and Infographics in presenting quantitative geographic information.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
Format Strengths (Advantages) Weaknesses (Limitations)
Map Displays spatial distribution across many locations. Can lack specific local details at varying scales.
Graph Clearly displays trends and changes over time. Can become confusing if too many variables are included.
Data Table Clearly and precisely organizes large numerical sets. Does not present information in a visual spatial format.
Infographic Presents complex data in an engaging, visual summary. Can oversimplify data and lack fine statistical detail.
💡 Memory Hook: Maps = Space, Graphs = Time, Tables = Numbers, Infographics = Visual summaries.
🚶 Section 3: Methods of Geographic Data Collection (Student pp. 20–22 / Key p. 2)
TOPIC 1.2 • FIELD OBSERVATION & LANDSCAPE ANALYSIS Describe
📖 Student Book: Pages 20–22 (Field Observation & Essential Question) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Describe the geographic data collection method of field observation and landscape analysis.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Field observation is the act of physically visiting a location to gather firsthand data through interviews, photography, and notes. Landscape analysis interprets the built and physical environment (building styles, agricultural patterns, street signs) to understand human-environment relationships."
💡 Memory Hook: Fieldwork = Boots on the ground! Visiting places in person and taking field notes.
TOPIC 1.2 • AERIAL PHOTOS VS. REMOTE SENSING Compare
📖 Student Book: Page 20 (Individual vs. Institutional Data) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Compare Aerial Photography with Remote Sensing as methods of geographic data collection.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"• Aerial Photography: Professional images captured by cameras mounted on airplanes or drones operating within the Earth's atmosphere.

• Remote Sensing: Data gathered by sensors and cameras mounted on satellites that orbit above the Earth's atmosphere in space."
💡 Memory Hook: Aerial = Airplanes in the air (Atmosphere). Remote Sensing = Satellites in Space!
TOPIC 1.2 • DATA COLLECTION SOURCES Compare
📖 Student Book: Page 20 (Individual vs. Institutional Sources Table) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Compare Individual Sources of geographic data with Institutional Sources (including tech companies and universities).
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"• Individual Sources: Data collected by single researchers or citizens via field observations, smartphone GPS tracking, and interviews.
• Institutional Sources: Large datasets gathered by government agencies (census bureaus), tech companies (smartphone locational app data), media outlets, and university researchers."
💡 Memory Hook: Individual = 1 person with a phone/notebook. Institutional = Government Census Bureau, Tech Apps, or UN.
🏛️ Section 4: Public Policy & Spatial Decision-Making (Student pp. 19–22 / Key pp. 4–5)
TOPIC 1.2 • URBAN PLANNING APPLICATION Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 19 (Census Data in Planning) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 2 (PDF Page 10)
Explain ONE way government census data is used by urban planners to make spatial decisions.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Urban planners analyze census demographic and income data to identify high-density, low-income neighborhoods where new public transit lines, public schools, emergency shelters, or subsidized healthcare clinics are most urgently needed."
💡 Memory Hook: Census numbers tell city planners where to spend tax dollars and build buses/schools.
TOPIC 1.2 • POLITICAL GEOGRAPHY IMPACT Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 19 (Political Consequences of Census) 🔑 Georgia GSE Alignment: SSWG1 / Political Processes
Explain ONE political consequence of census data collection in the United States.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"U.S. Census population counts determine the reapportionment of congressional seats in the U.S. House of Representatives among states and trigger the redrawing (redistricting) of legislative voting boundaries every ten years."
💡 Memory Hook: Census = Count People → Decide how many seats each state gets in Congress!
CHAPTER 1 FRQ CONNECTION • TOPIC 1.2 Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 28 (FRQ Stimulus Part C) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 4 (PDF Page 12)
Explain why a satellite photograph is classified as a qualitative data source rather than a quantitative source.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"A satellite photograph is a qualitative source because it provides a visual, descriptive representation of features without numerical measurements; in contrast, quantitative data requires recorded numerical statistics, counts, or measurements, which are not inherent in a visual photograph."
💡 Memory Hook: Photos = Qualitative (Visuals). Numbers = Quantitative (Counts).
