Identify the overall scale of the Earth at night image shown in the stimulus.

CHAPTER 1 FRQ • PART B Describe
📖 Student Book: Page 28 (FRQ Prompt B) & Page 17 (Spatial Patterns) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 4 (PDF Page 12)
Describe TWO spatial patterns visible on the global lights map.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"1. Coastal Clustering: The largest clusters of lights and population are concentrated along the ocean coasts of nearly all continents (e.g., eastern China, US Atlantic coast).

2. Development Disparity: Highly developed core regions (United States, Europe, Japan) are significantly brighter than less developed interior regions or sub-Saharan Africa."
💡 Memory Hook: Lights = Coasts (water transport) + Core Wealth (electricity & cities).
CHAPTER 1 FRQ • PART C Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 28 (FRQ Prompt C) & Pages 18–20 (Data Types) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 4 (PDF Page 12)
Explain why the Earth at night satellite image is considered a qualitative source and not quantitative.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"The Earth at night image is a qualitative source because it is a visual satellite photograph that provides a descriptive visual representation without numerical values; in contrast, quantitative data consists of measurable numerical counts and statistics (such as census population numbers), which are not present on the photograph."
💡 Memory Hook: Photos = Qualitative (visual description). Numbers/Tables = Quantitative (counts).
CHAPTER 1 FRQ • PART D Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 28 (FRQ Prompt D) & Pages 10, 14 (Physical Geography) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 5 (PDF Page 13)
Explain ONE reason why eastern China is significantly brighter than western China on the map.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Eastern China has favorable physical geography (fertile soils, fresh water rivers, and temperate climate) and economic situational advantages (coastal seaports and manufacturing hubs that attract massive populations), whereas western China is dominated by rugged mountains, deserts, and harsh arid climates that limit settlement."
💡 Memory Hook: East = Oceans, ports, factories, farmable land. West = Deserts and Himalayan mountains.
CHAPTER 1 FRQ • PART E Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 28 (FRQ Prompt E) & Pages 13, 24 (Trade & Relative Location) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 5 (PDF Page 13)
Explain ONE economic impact of having a high percentage of the world's population living along coastal regions.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Coastal locations provide direct access to deep-water seaports, leading to greater international trade, manufacturing growth, and higher economic wealth compared to interior provinces, while simultaneously driving up coastal property values and housing costs."
💡 Memory Hook: Coasts = Container ships → Global trade → Higher wealth and higher rent!
CHAPTER 1 FRQ • PART F Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 28 (FRQ Prompt F) & Pages 14, 23 (Human-Environment Impact) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 5 (PDF Page 13)
Explain ONE environmental impact of dense population concentrations along coastal zones.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"High coastal population density causes increased water, sewage, and industrial pollution, leading to the destruction of delicate coastal wetland habitats, coral reefs, and marine ecosystems through urban sprawl and shoreline development."
💡 Memory Hook: More people on the beach = Wetland destruction + Water & plastic runoff!
CHAPTER 1 FRQ • PART G Describe
📖 Student Book: Page 28 (FRQ Prompt G) & Pages 10, 24 (Map Inaccuracies) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 5 (PDF Page 13)
Describe a major limitation of using the Earth at night image to illustrate the location of the world's population.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"The image only reflects places with access to electrical infrastructure and the financial wealth to power lights at night; consequently, densely populated rural agricultural areas in less developed regions (particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia) appear dark and are severely underrepresented."
💡 Memory Hook: No electricity ≠ No people! Millions of people live in dark rural farm villages.

📐 Section 3: Thematic Map Classifications, Projections & SADD (Student pp. 14–17 / Key pp. 1–2)
TOPIC 1.1 • THEMATIC TYPES Identify
📖 Student Book: Pages 10–11 & Page 17 (Thematic Maps Table) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 1 (PDF Page 9)
Identify the difference between a Choropleth map and a Dot Distribution map.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"A choropleth map uses shades of color within predefined political borders (states/counties) to represent data averages, while a dot distribution map places individual dots at exact locations, showing spatial clustering and density without boundary constraints."
💡 Memory Hook: Choro = Color shading inside counties. Dots = Exact clusters anywhere on the map.
TOPIC 1.1 • PROJECTIONS & DISTORTION Compare
📖 Student Book: Pages 14–16 (Map Projections & Distortion) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 1 (PDF Page 9)
Compare the Mercator projection and Gall-Peters projection regarding the SADD distortion principle.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"The Mercator projection preserves direction and shape (true 90-degree angles for navigation) but severely distorts land area at the poles; the Gall-Peters projection is an equal-area projection that preserves accurate relative land area, but distorts shape by vertically stretching equatorial continents."
💡 Memory Hook: S.A.D.D. (Shape, Area, Distance, Direction). Mercator = good direction, bad area. Peters = good area, bad shape!
TOPIC 1.1 • SPATIAL PATTERNS Describe
📖 Student Book: Page 17 (Ways to Describe Spatial Patterns Table) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Pages 1–2 (PDF Pages 9–10)
Describe the differences between Clustered, Linear, Dispersed, and Geometric spatial patterns.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"• Clustered / Agglomerated: Features grouped closely together in a concentrated area.
• Linear: Features arranged in a straight continuous line (along a river or highway).
• Dispersed: Features spread out over a large area with no close grouping.
• Geometric / Grid: Features arranged in a regular, rectangular system (such as Township and Range)."
💡 Memory Hook: Clustered = Bunch, Linear = Line, Dispersed = Scattered, Geometric = Grid boxes.
