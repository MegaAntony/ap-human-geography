🏭 Section 1: The 5 Economic Sectors & Structural Transitions (Student pp. 453–455 / Key p. 84)
TOPIC 7.2 • 5 ECONOMIC SECTORS Identify
📖 Student Book: Page 453 (Structural Changes in Economies) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 84 (PDF Page 92)
Identify the five sectors of economic activity in order of increasing complexity.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"1. Primary Sector: Extraction of raw natural materials (agriculture, mining, forestry, fishing).
2. Secondary Sector: Manufacturing and processing raw materials into finished goods.
3. Tertiary Sector: Provision of services (retail, healthcare, hospitality, transportation).
4. Quaternary Sector: Knowledge and information activities (software engineering, financial planning, research).
5. Quinary Sector: Highest-level executive decision-making (CEOs, government leaders, top scientific researchers)."
💡 Memory Hook: 1=Dirt, 2=Making, 3=Serving, 4=Thinking, 5=Deciding.
TOPIC 7.2 • SECTORAL TRANSITIONS Describe
📖 Student Book: Pages 453–454 (Labor Force by Sector Graph) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 84 (PDF Page 92)
Describe the shift in economic sector employment that occurs as a country transitions to a post-industrial economy.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Primary sector agricultural employment drops to very low levels (<3%), secondary manufacturing employment declines due to automation and global outsourcing, while tertiary and quaternary service/knowledge sectors expand to employ over 75–80% of the total labor force."
💡 Memory Hook: Developing countries farm; developed countries sit in offices and write software!
OFFICIAL CHAPTER 18 FRQ • PART E Explain
📖 Student Book: Page 453 & Page 476 (Chapter 18 FRQ Part E) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 88 (PDF Page 96)
The number of people employed in the secondary sector in the United States has declined since 1975, but the overall production of goods has increased. Explain how this statement is true.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Companies replace human assembly line workers with advanced automation, industrial robots, and computerized manufacturing machinery that are far more cost-efficient, resulting in fewer factory workers while total production volume increases."
💡 Memory Hook: Robots make more cars in less time with fewer humans on the payroll!
📍 Section 2: Alfred Weber's Least Cost Theory & Locational Triangle (Student pp. 456–458 / Key p. 84)
TOPIC 7.2 • BULK-REDUCING INDUSTRY Define
📖 Student Book: Page 456 (Weber's Locational Triangle) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 84 (PDF Page 92)
Define Alfred Weber’s concept of a "bulk-reducing" (weight-losing) industry and state its optimal geographic location.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"A bulk-reducing industry is one where raw materials weigh MORE or are bulkier than the finished product. The optimal factory location is near the raw material source to minimize expensive transportation freight costs on heavy raw inputs.

Examples: Copper smelting, paper mills, and sawmills."
💡 Memory Hook: Bulk-Reducing = Loses weight in the factory $\rightarrow$ Build factory NEXT TO THE MINE/TREES!
TOPIC 7.2 • BULK-GAINING INDUSTRY Define
📖 Student Book: Page 456 (Weber's Locational Triangle) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 84 (PDF Page 92)
Define a "bulk-gaining" (weight-gaining) industry and state its optimal geographic location.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"A bulk-gaining industry is one where the finished product weighs MORE, is bulkier, or is more fragile than the raw material components. The optimal factory location is near the consumer market.

Examples: Soft drink bottling (heavy water added locally), automobile assembly, and commercial bakeries."
💡 Memory Hook: Bulk-Gaining = Gets heavier/bigger $\rightarrow$ Build factory NEXT TO THE CUSTOMER/MARKET!
TOPIC 7.2 • AGGLOMERATION & BREAK-OF-BULK Explain
📖 Student Book: Pages 457–458 (Industrial Agglomeration) 🔑 Teacher Answer Key: Page 83 (PDF Page 91)
Explain the economic benefit of "agglomeration" and describe the function of a "break-of-bulk point."
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"• Agglomeration: The geographic clustering of related businesses (e.g., Silicon Valley) allows firms to share specialized infrastructure, supplier networks, and skilled talent pools, reducing operational costs.

• Break-of-Bulk Point: A location where cargo is transferred from one transportation mode to another (e.g., ocean container ship to rail/truck at deep-water seaports like the Port of Savannah)."
💡 Memory Hook: Agglomeration = Clustering together to save money. Break-of-Bulk = Where big ships unload onto trains/trucks.

Explain why traditional global industrial regions are concentrated along coastlines, major navigable rivers, and near large urban complexes.
⭐ Exactly What to Write on Your Exam:
"Waterways allow for inexpensive bulk transportation of raw materials and finished goods by barge and cargo ship; locations within urban complexes provide direct access to a large labor supply, a large local consumer market, and established transport networks."
